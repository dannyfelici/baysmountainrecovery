## What Is Addiction?

Addiction, also called a substance use disorder, is a medical condition that affects a person's ability to control their use of alcohol or drugs, even when that use causes serious problems. It can involve strong cravings, physical dependence, changes in behavior, and continued substance use despite harmful consequences.

Addiction can affect people from every background. It isn't a sign of weakness or a lack of willpower. Substance use disorders involve complex interactions between physical health, mental health, personal experiences, and environmental factors.

With professional treatment and ongoing support, people struggling with addiction can learn to manage their condition and build a life in recovery.

## When Is It Time to Seek Addiction Treatment?

Recognizing that you may need help isn't always easy. You might tell yourself that your substance use isn't serious enough to require treatment, or that you'll be able to stop whenever you're ready. However, addiction often becomes more difficult to manage over time.

You may benefit from professional addiction treatment if you:

* Have difficulty controlling how much or how often you drink or use drugs.
* Experience strong cravings or feel preoccupied with obtaining or using substances.
* Need increasing amounts of a substance to experience the same effects.
* Experience withdrawal symptoms when you stop or reduce substance use.
* Have tried to quit or cut back but repeatedly return to using.
* Continue using substances despite problems with your health, relationships, or responsibilities.
* Use alcohol or drugs to cope with stress, anxiety, depression, or difficult emotions.
* Spend increasing amounts of time obtaining, using, or recovering from substances.

You don't have to experience every one of these signs to need help. You also don't have to reach a crisis point before seeking treatment. If alcohol or drugs are interfering with your life, professional support can help you explore your options.

## Types of Addiction We Treat

Different substances affect the brain and body in different ways. Some can cause dangerous physical withdrawal symptoms, while others may contribute to significant changes in mood, sleep, behavior, or mental health.

At Bays Mountain Recovery, we recognize that addiction treatment must account for both the substance involved and the person struggling with its effects. Our programs help adults address substance use disorders through individualized care and recovery support.

### Alcohol Addiction Treatment

Alcohol addiction can develop gradually, making it difficult to recognize when drinking has become a serious problem. You may find yourself drinking more than intended, struggling to cut back, or relying on alcohol to manage stress and difficult emotions.

Prolonged heavy drinking can also lead to physical dependence. For some people, suddenly stopping alcohol use can cause dangerous withdrawal symptoms, including seizures.

Bays Mountain Recovery offers medical detox and residential care for adults struggling with alcohol use disorder.

### Opioid Addiction Treatment

Opioid addiction can involve prescription pain medications, synthetic opioids such as fentanyl, and other substances in the opioid family. These drugs can cause physical dependence, intense cravings, and changes in the brain that make stopping difficult.

Opioid use disorder also carries a significant risk of overdose, particularly with fentanyl and other highly potent opioids.

Treatment helps address physical dependence, patterns of substance use, and the challenges of maintaining recovery.

### Heroin Addiction Treatment

Heroin is an opioid that can cause dependence and addiction. People who use heroin may experience powerful cravings, difficult withdrawal symptoms, and an increased risk of overdose, particularly when the drug supply contains fentanyl or other unexpected substances.

Heroin addiction can affect physical health, emotional well-being, relationships, and daily responsibilities. Professional treatment can provide structure and support while helping you address the behaviors and circumstances associated with substance use.

### Methamphetamine Addiction Treatment

Methamphetamine is a powerful stimulant that affects the central nervous system. Repeated use can contribute to changes in mood, sleep, appetite, behavior, and overall health.

People struggling with methamphetamine addiction may experience strong cravings, exhaustion, depression, or other emotional difficulties when they stop using. These challenges can make recovery difficult without consistent support.

Residential addiction treatment provides an opportunity to step away from substance use triggers and begin developing healthier habits and coping strategies.

## Our Approach to Drug and Alcohol Addiction Treatment

Addiction affects more than your physical health. It can influence how you think, respond to stress, manage relationships, and cope with difficult experiences. Effective treatment addresses these challenges while helping you develop the skills needed for recovery.

At Bays Mountain Recovery, our approach begins with understanding your individual needs and creating a treatment plan that supports your recovery goals.

### Individualized Treatment Planning

No two people experience addiction in exactly the same way. Your substance use history, physical and mental health, personal circumstances, and previous treatment experiences all play a role in determining what kind of care may be appropriate.

An individualized treatment plan helps ensure that your recovery addresses the challenges most relevant to you.

### Therapeutic Support and Recovery Skills

Addiction treatment involves more than stopping alcohol or drug use. Through [addiction therapy and counseling](/therapies/), you can begin exploring the thoughts, emotions, and behaviors associated with substance use.

Treatment may help you recognize triggers, manage cravings, develop healthier coping strategies, improve communication, and respond to stress without relying on substances.

### Planning for Continued Recovery

Recovery is an ongoing process that extends beyond your time in treatment. Preparing for life after rehab may involve identifying sources of support, developing relapse prevention strategies, and establishing healthier daily routines.

The goal is to help you leave treatment with a stronger understanding of your addiction and practical tools for continuing your recovery.

## Medical Detox and Residential Addiction Treatment

Bays Mountain Recovery offers two connected levels of care for adults struggling with substance use disorders. The appropriate starting point depends on your substance use history, physical health, withdrawal risks, and individual treatment needs.

### Medical Detox

For some people, addiction treatment begins with [medical detox](/detox/). This level of care focuses on managing withdrawal symptoms, monitoring physical health, and helping the body stabilize after prolonged substance use.

Medical detox is especially important for people who may experience dangerous withdrawal symptoms from alcohol, benzodiazepines, or certain other substances.

Although detox can be an important first step, it does not address every aspect of addiction. Continued treatment is often necessary to support lasting recovery.

### Residential Treatment

Our [residential treatment program](/residential-treatment/) provides a structured, live-in environment where adults can focus on overcoming addiction. Treatment addresses the emotional, behavioral, and psychological challenges associated with substance use while helping patients develop healthier habits and recovery skills.

Because Bays Mountain Recovery offers medical detox and residential treatment at the same Kingsport facility, patients who need both levels of care may be able to transition directly from withdrawal management into continued addiction treatment.

This connected approach helps reduce disruption during a vulnerable time and allows patients to continue focusing on recovery in a familiar environment.

## Treating Addiction and Co-Occurring Mental Health Conditions

Substance use disorders frequently occur alongside mental health challenges such as anxiety, depression, and trauma-related symptoms. For some people, alcohol or drugs become a way to cope with emotional pain or overwhelming experiences. Over time, substance use can make these symptoms worse, creating a cycle that becomes difficult to manage.

Bays Mountain Recovery provides addiction treatment for adults with co-occurring mental health conditions. Our approach recognizes that substance use and mental health can influence one another and that recovery may require support for both.
